# RACH Profiles — editorial plan

Status: Active weekly series — pilot published

Last updated: August 28, 2026

Public series name: **RACH Profiles**
Working promise: **The life stories of people building what comes next.**

## Purpose

RACH stands for **Recognizing the Achievements and Contributions to Humanity**. RACH Profiles is a weekly long-form series about consequential and unusually interesting people in artificial intelligence, technology, construction, and longevity or biotechnology.

The public purpose is to recognize achievement, explain how meaningful work is built, and give readers useful lessons. Relationship and growth potential may inform internal subject selection, but it must not turn a profile into a hidden sales approach, paid endorsement, or approval-controlled tribute.

The editorial stance is **critical admiration**: generous about demonstrated achievement, exact about evidence, candid about failure and criticism, and clear about what remains an ambition.

## Quarterly rotation

The initial 12-week season uses a repeating four-week rhythm:

1. Artificial Intelligence
2. Construction and the Built Environment
3. Technology
4. Longevity and Biotechnology

Cross-disciplinary subjects receive one primary category and up to two secondary categories. The primary category controls the rotation.

## Subject selection score

Score candidates before commissioning. A high score does not override a privacy, conflict, rights, litigation, or evidence-risk failure.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Demonstrated impact | 20 |
| Narrative depth | 20 |
| Reader relevance | 15 |
| Original editorial angle | 15 |
| Evidence availability | 15 |
| Timeliness | 10 |
| Interview and visual potential | 5 |

## Standard article package

Target 3,500–5,000 words and a 15–22 minute read. Do not pad a thin record to imitate depth.

Each published profile should normally contain:

- A precise headline, deck, byline, editor or fact-checker, publication date, modified date, and reporting cutoff.
- A rights-cleared profile picture with useful alt text, creator credit, source, license or permission basis, and crop or edit record.
- A concise “Why RACH recognizes this person” statement on the series card and a fuller, evidence-backed recognition section in the biography.
- A documented opening moment rather than an invented scene.
- Origins and formative influences that are relevant, verified, and not needlessly invasive.
- Apprenticeship, setbacks, and course corrections.
- The major work or inflection point, with collaborators and predecessors properly credited.
- Methods and leadership style, distinguishing self-description from independent observation.
- Measured impact, with first-party metrics attributed and dated.
- Material criticism, limitations, failures, or competing interpretations.
- Current roles checked immediately before publication.
- Evidence-connected lessons labeled as RACH editorial analysis.
- A timeline, source list, methodology note, interview status, disclosures, image credit and license, and correction route.

Minimum reporting target:

- 12–25 substantive sources.
- At least three primary records.
- At least two credible independent sources.
- One interview with the subject or a documented invitation and attempt.
- An independent or critical perspective when important claims are contested.
- A documented rights basis for every image.

## Evidence language

Use these internal claim codes:

| Code | Meaning | Publication treatment |
|---|---|---|
| F | Verified fact | State directly and cite. |
| A | Attributed claim | Name the source: “the company reports…” |
| AN | Editorial analysis or inference | Label the judgment and show its evidence. |
| O | Opinion | Attribute it to the speaker or RACH. |
| P | Prediction or incomplete plan | Use “plans,” “expects,” or “aims,” with a date. |
| D | Disputed | Describe the disagreement and strongest evidence fairly. |
| U | Unknown or unverified | Omit or say it could not be verified. |

Source order:

1. Primary records: interviews, filings, regulatory records, papers, patents, project records, speeches, and original audio or video.
2. Independent authoritative reporting and academic or professional sources.
3. First-party institutional material, clearly treated as what the organization says.
4. Tertiary sources only to discover better evidence.

Every surprising, reputation-defining, disputed, or potentially damaging claim requires strong primary evidence plus independent corroboration where possible. Never use AI output as evidence.

## Living-person and health guardrails

- Include personal facts only when verified, publicly relevant to the work, and proportionate to foreseeable harm.
- Exclude private addresses, personal contact details, children's identities, private health information, and speculative net worth.
- Do not diagnose personality, health, neurodivergence, or mental state.
- Distinguish allegation, lawsuit, regulatory finding, charge, and conviction.
- Offer a meaningful right of reply for adverse material.
- Escalate misconduct allegations, active litigation, leaked material, disputed credit, and serious medical or financial assertions for qualified review before publication.
- Longevity articles must label the evidence level: mechanism, cell study, animal study, observational human study, randomized trial, systematic review, guideline, or regulatory decision.
- A biomarker shift, model prediction, animal result, or early trial is not evidence of longer human life.
- Profiles are educational editorial work, not medical advice.

## Image and licensing rules

- Use commissioned, public-domain, Creative Commons, press-permission, or directly licensed images only.
- Every published profile requires a real, rights-cleared portrait of the subject on both the series card and the biography page. If no suitable portrait is cleared, keep the profile in draft rather than using an unlicensed image or an AI-generated likeness as documentary photography.
- Retain the source URL, creator, date, license, attribution language, edit or crop record, and any personality-rights notice.
- Do not assume that an image appearing on an official site is reusable.
- Label AI-generated or reconstructed visuals; never present them as documentary evidence.
- Use the subject image for article-specific Open Graph metadata only when the rights record supports that use.

## Workflow and authority states

The required state sequence is:

`Candidate → Approved for research → Reporting → Draft → Internal fact-check → Outreach approved → Subject fact-check sent → Final review → Approved to publish → Published → Updated or corrected`

These states are not interchangeable:

- **Draft** is not fact-checked.
- **Fact-checked** does not mean the subject approved it.
- **Subject response received** does not transfer editorial control.
- **Approved to publish** requires an explicit internal decision.
- **Published** requires verification of the actual public page.

No interview invitation, fact-check memorandum, image request, or other external message should be sent without approval of the exact subject and copy. No profile should be pushed or deployed merely because a local page builds successfully.

## Rolling three-week cadence

### T–21 to T–15 — Commission

- Score and approve the subject.
- Screen conflicts, privacy, rights, litigation, and evidentiary risk.
- Define the central question, likely opening, reporting plan, and image path.
- Approve interview outreach.

### T–14 to T–10 — Report

- Read primary sources first.
- Conduct subject and independent interviews.
- Build the timeline, source map, claim ledger, contradictions list, and unanswered questions.
- Confirm that the evidence is deep enough for a full profile.

### T–9 to T–7 — Draft

- Write a one-page narrative outline.
- Draft with claim IDs or source markers attached.
- Write lessons only after the evidence sections are complete.

### T–6 to T–4 — Verify and seek response

- Check every material sentence against the original source.
- Recheck current roles and quantitative claims.
- Send a targeted fact-check memorandum with a reasonable deadline.
- Finish rights clearance and escalate legal or ethical issues.

### T–3 to T–1 — Edit and package

- Incorporate supported corrections.
- Complete structural, line, fairness, and copy edits.
- Validate links, citations, metadata, structured data, image credits, accessibility, and mobile layout.
- Prepare newsletter and social derivatives from approved claims only.
- Obtain explicit approval to publish.

### Publication and follow-up

- Verify the canonical public page, metadata, links, and social preview.
- Send or post only approved derivatives.
- Monitor correction requests and broken links.
- Review performance at seven and thirty days.
- Reverify evergreen profiles after major role changes and at least annually.

## Weekly distribution package

Derivatives may not introduce claims absent from the approved article.

- 150–300 word LinkedIn post.
- 6–10 post Threads or X sequence.
- 8–10 panel carousel.
- 30–60 second vertical-video script and 90–180 second extended script.
- 3–5 fact or quote cards.
- 120–180 word newsletter introduction.
- Five approved pull quotes.
- Internal relationship note kept outside the public article.

Measure engaged reading, completion, saves, substantive shares, newsletter conversion, earned links, relevant inbound relationships, sourcing completeness, and correction rate—not impressions alone.

## Pilot publication record

The editorial owner explicitly authorized publication of the Demis Hassabis profile on August 28, 2026. Its claim ledger records the publication-day role and clinical-registry checks, science and fairness reviews, collaborator-credit additions, Creative Commons portrait rights, exact image modifications, reporting cutoff, and release metadata.

No interview or fact-check request was sent to Hassabis or any associated organization. The published article must therefore continue to identify itself as an independent public-record biography that is neither authorized by nor endorsed by the subject. This documented pilot exception does not remove right-of-reply review from the standard workflow for future profiles, especially where adverse, disputed, private, or high-risk claims are proposed.

The pilot was published through the existing Cloudflare Pages production pipeline on August 28, 2026. The canonical hub, article, portrait, structured data, indexability, sitemap, `www` route, and deployment route were verified after release. The exact commit and deployment identifiers are retained in the profile claim ledger.
